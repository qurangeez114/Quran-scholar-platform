📖 How to read this page
Tap to expand · 4 steps
1
Each card = one recitation tradition
The Quran was memorized and passed down through 10 canonical reciters, each through 2 chains — 20 valid readings in total. This page shows 8 of those 20 with full text. They are all considered equally authentic — not "versions," but the same Quran transmitted with slight pronunciation or word-form variations.
Blue = Ḥafṣ (baseline) Amber = Warsh Green = Qālūn Purple = Al-Dūrī Rose = Shuʿbah Teal = Al-Sūsī Pink = Al-Bazzī Violet = Qunbul
2
Colored + underlined words differ from the baseline
Every word is compared against Ḥafṣ (the blue baseline). If a word in any of the other 7 transmissions differs from Ḥafṣ — even slightly — it appears in that riwayah's color with an underline.

Tap or hover a colored word to see a tooltip with its lexical meaning in English — for example, hovering مَلِكِ shows "King / Sovereign (one who rules)".
💡 Most differences are phonetic or orthographic — the same word written or pronounced slightly differently. Only a small number (19 out of 6,236 verses) are meaning-changing — these are marked ★ and explained in the Significant tab.
3
Read the status badges
Each card has a badge in the top-right corner:
✓ Baseline = This is Ḥafṣ — the reference reading
= Same = Identical consonantal text to Ḥafṣ
≠ Differs = At least one word is different
💡 "Same" doesn't mean identical characters — Arabic script varies between mushaf styles. It means the consonants and meaning are the same; only calligraphy, vowel marks, or pause symbols differ.
4
Use the summary box and AI explain
At the top of each result, a "What differs" box shows every differing word with both Arabic forms and an English gloss side by side — so you can see the difference at a glance without reading Arabic.

For any card marked ≠ Differs, tap "🤖 Explain in plain English" to get a 2–3 sentence scholarly explanation of what the word difference means and whether it matters for law, theology, or narrative.
💡 Start with the ★ chips above (e.g. "1:4 Mālik vs Malik") — these are the most interesting examples where the reading tradition genuinely changes the shade of meaning.

Islamic scholarship recognizes 10 canonical reciters (qurrāʾ), each transmitted through 2 narrators — 20 riwayat in total, all considered equally authentic. This page currently has full verse text for 8 transmissions (marked ✓). The remaining 12 are documented below with their scholarly characteristics and external reference links.

Ḥafṣ ʿan ʿĀṣim ʿĀṣim al-KūfīḤafṣKufa · Worldwide ✓ Full text Most widely used today (90%+ of Muslims). The standard Egyptian printed Quran (1924) follows this reading. Quran.com ↗
Shuʿbah ʿan ʿĀṣim ʿĀṣim al-KūfīShuʿbahKufa · Historical ✓ Full text Shares ʿĀṣim as reader with Ḥafṣ, yet diverges in ~666 word positions. Notable: diverges from Ḥafṣ at 21:4 (قُلْ vs قَالَ) and 28:48. Islamway ↗
Warsh ʿan Nāfiʿ Nāfiʿ al-MadanīWarsh (ʿUthmān b. Saʿīd)Madinah → N. & W. Africa ✓ Full text Standard in Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Mauritania, West Africa. Notable for elongated vowels (al-madd) and hamza handling. 2:184 has plural masākīn vs Ḥafṣ singular. Tanzil ↗ · Audio ↗
Qālūn ʿan Nāfiʿ Nāfiʿ al-MadanīQālūn (ʿĪsā b. Minnāʾ)Madinah → Libya, Tunisia ✓ Full text Standard in Libya and parts of Tunisia. Differs from Warsh in hamza pronunciation and some qaṣr/madd choices despite sharing Nāfiʿ as reader. Tanzil ↗
Al-Dūrī ʿan Abī ʿAmr Abū ʿAmr al-BaṣrīAl-Dūrī (Ḥafṣ b. ʿUmar)Basra → Sudan, E. Africa ✓ Full text Standard in Sudan, Somalia, Eritrea, parts of Nigeria. Key legal difference: 5:6 reads arjulikum (wipe feet) vs Ḥafṣ arjulakum (wash). 2:222 reads yattahharna (active purification required). Audio ↗
Al-Sūsī ʿan Abī ʿAmr Abū ʿAmr al-BaṣrīAl-SūsīBasra → Historical ✓ Full text Shares Abū ʿAmr as reader with Al-Dūrī. Differs in imāla (vowel inclination) and some imalā forms. Historically used in Andalusia. Quranpedia ↗
Al-Bazzī ʿan Ibn Kathīr Ibn Kathīr al-MakkīAl-BazzīMecca → Historical ✓ Full text Meccan reading. Notable for tashqīq al-hamza (double hamza pronunciation) and specific basmalah treatment. 9:100 reads "min taḥtihā" with extra preposition. Quranpedia ↗
Qunbul ʿan Ibn Kathīr Ibn Kathīr al-MakkīQunbulMecca → Historical ✓ Full text Shares Ibn Kathīr as reader with Al-Bazzī. Diverges in some imāla and madd forms. 5:6 reads arjulikum (genitive — wipe feet). Quranpedia ↗
Hishām ʿan Ibn ʿĀmir Ibn ʿĀmir al-ShāmīHishāmDamascus → Historical Pending Syrian reading. 2:259 reads nunshiruhā (revive/resurrect bones) vs Ḥafṣ nunshizuhā (raise/assemble). 3:146 reads qutila (prophets martyred, passive) vs Ḥafṣ qātala (prophets fought, active). Quranpedia ↗
Ibn Dhakwān ʿan Ibn ʿĀmir Ibn ʿĀmir al-ShāmīIbn DhakwānDamascus → Historical Pending Shares Ibn ʿĀmir as reader with Hishām. 2:184 reads ṭaʿāmi masākīna (plural — feed several poor persons per missed fast). Quranpedia ↗
Khalaf ʿan Ḥamzah Ḥamzah al-KūfīKhalafKufa → Historical Pending 5:6 reads arjulikum (genitive — wipe feet in wudu). 2:222 reads yattahharna (requires active ghusl). Known for distinctive hamza-handling: tahqīq al-hamzāt. Quranpedia ↗
Khallād ʿan Ḥamzah Ḥamzah al-KūfīKhallādKufa → Historical Pending Shares Ḥamzah as reader with Khalaf. 21:4 reads qul (imperative) vs Ḥafṣ qāla. 43:19 reads ʿinda al-Raḥmān (nearness) vs Ḥafṣ ʿibādu al-Raḥmān (servanthood). Quranpedia ↗
Abū al-Ḥārith ʿan al-Kisāʾī Al-Kisāʾī al-KūfīAbū al-ḤārithKufa → Historical Pending Known for imāla (vowel inclination toward "i"). 2:222 reads yattahharna (intensive Form V — ghusl required). 1:4 reads mālikī (owner, like Ḥafṣ). 21:4 reads qul (imperative). Quranpedia ↗
Al-Dūrī ʿan al-Kisāʾī Al-Kisāʾī al-KūfīAl-DūrīKufa → Historical Pending Same narrator (Al-Dūrī) transmits two separate reading chains — one from Abū ʿAmr (above), one from al-Kisāʾī. A remarkable case of a single narrator bridging two distinct reading traditions. Quranpedia ↗
Ibn Wardān ʿan Abī Jaʿfar Abū Jaʿfar al-MadanīIbn WardānMadinah → Historical Pending One of the two "extra" readers beyond the famous seven, included in Ibn al-Jazarī's ten. Characterized by idghām (assimilation) in specific contexts different from the seven. Al-Nashr ↗
Ibn Jammāz ʿan Abī Jaʿfar Abū Jaʿfar al-MadanīIbn JammāzMadinah → Historical Pending Shares Abū Jaʿfar as reader with Ibn Wardān. 66:12 reads kitābihi (singular — Maryam's faith in one book) vs Ḥafṣ kutubihi (plural — all revealed books). Al-Nashr ↗
Ruways ʿan Yaʿqūb Yaʿqūb al-ḤaḍramīRuwaysBasra → Historical Pending Basran reading included in the ten. Known for specific waqf (pause) traditions and some distinctive imalā forms. 19:25 reads yassāqaṭ (masculine subject — the trunk sheds dates). Quranpedia ↗
Rawḥ ʿan Yaʿqūb Yaʿqūb al-ḤaḍramīRawḥBasra → Historical Pending Shares Yaʿqūb as reader with Ruways. Characterized by full pronunciation of final vowels (ṣilat al-hāʾ) and specific madd lengths. 18:86 reads ḥāmiyatin (hot spring) vs Ḥafṣ ḥamiʾatin (muddy spring). Quranpedia ↗
Isḥāq ʿan Khalaf al-ʿĀshir Khalaf al-ʿĀshirIsḥāqBaghdad → Historical Pending Khalaf al-ʿĀshir is himself a transmitter of Ḥamzah, but also counted as an independent reader (the "tenth"). 5:6 reads arjulikum (wipe feet). 43:19 reads ʿinda al-Raḥmān. Al-Nashr ↗
Idrīs ʿan Khalaf al-ʿĀshir Khalaf al-ʿĀshirIdrīsBaghdad → Historical Pending Shares Khalaf al-ʿĀshir as reader with Isḥāq. 21:4 reads qul (imperative — God commands). 2:9 reads yukhādiʿūna (Form III mutual deception) vs Ḥafṣ Form I. Al-Nashr ↗
