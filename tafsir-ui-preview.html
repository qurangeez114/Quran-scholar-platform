Scholar Summary

Mary’s people address her by the phrase “sister of Aaron,” and the classical commentaries preserve more than one explanation of what that phrase means.

The main interpretive question is not whether Mary had a literal biological brother named Aaron. The sources discuss identity, association, lineage, naming practice, and rhetorical comparison. A reader can understand the issue here first, then open each scholar only when deeper evidence is wanted.

Where the sources agree

✓ Shared interpretive frame

The verse records the accusation made by Mary’s people. They contrast her respected family background with what they believe they are seeing, which explains why both her father and mother are invoked in the rebuke.

Agreement does not mean every commentator explains “Aaron” in the same way.
Where interpretation branches

⚖ The phrase “sister of Aaron”

This is the interpretive fork the reader should see before opening six long commentaries.

Explanation A

“Aaron” can refer to a righteous or well-known person among them, with Mary being associated with him by naming, religious resemblance, or communal expression.

Explanation B

Other transmitted explanations connect the phrase to ancestry, kinship language, or an Aaron belonging to a different generation rather than treating the wording as a simple biological-sibling statement.

Important: the platform should label each report by scholar and attribution rather than present one option as though all six sources say exactly the same thing.
