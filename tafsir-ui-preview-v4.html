“O sister of Aaron! Your father was not an evil man, nor was your mother unchaste.”
Who is “Aaron”?

This page separates what a commentator states, what he merely transmits, and what he rejects. The counts below mean “sources that preserve this interpretation,” not votes for what six scholars personally believed.

Righteousness / resemblance — 5 sources

Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, Qurṭubī, Jalālayn, and Tanwīr al-Miqbās preserve a reading in which Mary is linked to a righteous Aaron by resemblance, worship, chastity, or communal naming language.

Contemporary brother — 3 sources

Qurṭubī, al-Saʿdī, and Tanwīr al-Miqbās preserve a reading in which Aaron is a real person from Mary’s own generation. Al-Saʿdī presents this as the apparent meaning and explicitly distinguishes him from Aaron, brother of Moses.

Lineage / descent — 3 sources

Ṭabarī, Ibn Kathīr, and Qurṭubī preserve a broader kinship reading: Mary is associated with Aaron, brother of Moses, through descent or ancestral/tribal language rather than being his literal contemporary sister.

Wicked-man comparison — 2 sources

Ibn Kathīr and Tanwīr al-Miqbās preserve a report that “Aaron” was a wicked man and Mary was compared to him. This is a transmitted minority report, not automatically the commentator’s own adopted view.

Special case: Ibn Kathīr also transmits a report that Mary was literally the sister of Moses and Aaron, then explicitly rejects it as chronologically impossible. It is therefore shown as “rejected,” not counted as Ibn Kathīr’s belief.
