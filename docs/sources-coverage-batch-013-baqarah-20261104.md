## Source comparisons
**Q 2:115 and Isaiah 45:6** ([Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.45.6); [Hebrew lexicon](https://biblehub.com/lexicon/isaiah/45-6.htm)). Both deploy east–west imagery for God's sovereignty, but Isaiah concerns recognition of YHWH as sole God from sunrise to sunset; the Qur'an states that east and west belong to God and describes God's face wherever one turns. **Loose motif only.** Do not claim a shared ritual orientation command.

**Q 2:116 and John 3:16–18** ([ESV primary passage](https://www.esv.org/John%2B3%3A16%E2%80%9318/)). The Johannine text describes God's unique Son sent for salvation; Q 2:116 denies that God has taken a son. **Doctrinal contrast, not a matching narrative** and not proof the Qur'anic speaker had the Gospel of John open. The precise semantic range of Greek *monogenēs*, the Qur'anic Arabic, and the theology of sonship require independent specialist review. Do not misrepresent Christian sonship as necessarily physical reproduction.
