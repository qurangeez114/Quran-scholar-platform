# QuranHikma historical research draft — Āl ʿImrān 3:21–30

**2026-11-05 · unpublished, preliminary · not a public Sources card**

## Passage-level historical orientation
The ten verses move from condemnation of those who kill prophets and people advocating justice (3:21–22), to disputation about scriptural authority and judgment (3:23–25), then to God's sovereign disposition of power and natural cycles (3:26–27), allegiance and divine knowledge (3:28–30). A thematic resemblance is not evidence of transmission. The historical dossiers must distinguish the late-antique Qur'anic rhetorical setting from the literary settings of biblical sources.

## Additional witnesses to investigate — not staged associations
- **1 Kings 19:10:** Elijah's complaint that Israelites killed prophets. Distinct speech situation; not necessarily a direct textual parallel.
- **2 Chronicles 24:20–22:** account of the stoning of Zechariah son of Jehoiada; a concrete narrative example, not an automatic identification of the unnamed victims in Qur'an 3:21.
- **Luke 11:47–51:** accusation about ancestors killing prophets; investigate Greek and its own polemical setting before comparing.
- **Daniel 2:21:** God changes times and removes/sets up kings, potentially relevant to 3:26. Check Aramaic, translation and exact imagery before recording a candidate.
- **3:24:** 'numbered days' of punishment requires source-specific Jewish texts and dates, not a generic claim about Jewish doctrine.

## Chronology, textual transmission and limits
The narrative and prophetic books of the Hebrew Bible reflect layered ancient compositions and redaction, not a single date. Nehemiah 9's prayer is presented in a Persian-period narrative setting; that narrative setting must not be mistaken for a securely established final composition date. Hebrew Masoretic textual witnesses and Greek translation traditions require comparison at the passage level. Modern English Bible publication dates are not ancient composition or manuscript dates. The Qur'an's 7th-century setting is later than the broad biblical traditions, but temporal priority alone cannot demonstrate copying or direct access.

## Interpretive alternatives
A shared inherited prophetic criticism tradition, independent use of a broad ethical motif, and more specific intertextual engagement are distinct hypotheses. None is established by this initial English-text comparison. The phrase about killing those who command justice is a meaningful Qur'anic expansion and must not be silently attributed to Nehemiah.
