## Main research question: Q 2:102, Solomon and magic
The Qur'anic Arabic passage explicitly dissociates Solomon from disbelief and places the teaching of magic with devils and Harut/Marut at Babylon. [Qur'an 2:102](https://quran.com/2/102).

### Earlier textual comparisons
1. [1 Kings 11:1–13](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Kings.11.1-13), Hebrew Masoretic text and JPS translation: Solomon is charged with turning after other gods. This is a **contrast of religious characterization**, not an account of Babylonian angelic magic; Kings does not name Harut or Marut.
2. [Deuteronomy 18:9–14](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.18.9-14), Hebrew Masoretic text/JPS: prohibition of divination and sorcery. This is a **legal motif**, not a shared episode or proof of borrowing.
3. [Psalm 83:4–5](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.83.4-5) as a *very loose* analogy for Q 2:109: different community, setting and narrative; likely exclude from Sources after review.

### Historical dossier plan
**Solomon, magic and Babylon**: compare Kings 11, Deuteronomy 18, securely dated Jewish and late-antique magical Solomon traditions, and (only after critical-edition dating and witness review) the Testament of Solomon. Separate the historical Solomon, later pseudepigraphal attributions, Qur'anic polemic, and later Muslim tafsir. Trace Hebrew/Greek/Syriac witnesses where available, document original language, edition, composition versus surviving manuscript dates, geographical diffusion and alternative scholarly interpretations. Investigate proposed Iranian analogues to Harut/Marut only through dated primary attestations, not etymological speculation. This document is an **unpublished research plan**, not a completed scholarly dossier.
