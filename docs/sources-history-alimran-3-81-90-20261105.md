## Context and narrative sequence
Qur'an 3:81 sets out a covenant involving prophets, scripture, wisdom and support for a confirming messenger. 3:82 treats rejection of that covenant; 3:83–85 address universal submission, named prophetic revelations and religious identity. 3:86–90 discuss rejection after belief, judgment, repentance and renewed/increased unbelief. The text is argumentative and theological, not a discrete historical episode; avoid inventing a location, date, named interlocutor or pre-Islamic story.

**Scope discipline:** The Hebrews passage is a single shared source passage associated provisionally with multiple Qur'anic verses; it is not four independent textual discoveries. 3:82–85, 3:87–88 have no specific primary-text parallel established *in this preliminary pass*; this does not assert that none exists.

## Chronology and transmission
Deuteronomy is a Hebrew biblical text with a long and layered composition and transmission history, preceding Islam; its final literary formation cannot be equated with the dates of its extant manuscripts. Compare Masoretic, Septuagint and relevant Qumran witnesses where attested for the precise passage. Hebrews is a first-century CE Greek Christian composition; extant papyri and later codices postdate its composition. Verify the specific text in a critical edition and distinguish earliest extant witness for the *passage* from general manuscript dates. Modern online translations are not themselves ancient manuscript witnesses.

## Interpretive alternatives
Deuteronomy 18 has been read within Jewish tradition in connection with prophetic authority and within Christian tradition in relation to Jesus; subsequent Muslim identification with Muhammad is a reception-history claim to document with dated sources, not a claim that Deuteronomy directly names him. Hebrews 6 has varied Christian readings concerning apostasy and repentance. The Qur'anic difference between 3:89 and 3:90 must be retained rather than treating the entire unit as a single absolute prohibition of repentance. None of these thematic correspondences proves copying, direct literary dependence or a single transmission route.
