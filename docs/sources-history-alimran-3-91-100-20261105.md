## Historical setting and source chronology

This passage moves from eschatological ransom and charitable righteousness (3:91–92), through dietary claims associated with Israel and Torah (3:93–95), to the sanctuary at Bakkah, Abraham's standing-place and pilgrimage (3:96–97), then polemic with People of the Book (3:98–100). The Quranic composition belongs to the seventh-century Arabian milieu; the Hebrew Bible contains older literary traditions with complex editorial histories and surviving witnesses substantially later than many proposed composition stages. A manuscript's date is not the same as the date of its underlying composition. No transmission route to the Qur'anic text is established by motif comparison alone.

## Verse-specific screening

- **3:91:** Ransom imagery; Psalm 49 is only a possible thematic comparison.
- **3:92:** Giving cherished possessions; Deuteronomy 15 is a broad moral comparison, not an identical command.
- **3:93:** Israel's pre-Torah self-restriction; Genesis 32 is a research lead, not an established explanation of the Qur'anic statement.
- **3:94:** False attribution to God; no sufficiently specific earlier passage identified in this pass.
- **3:95:** Abrahamic monotheism; no newly specific passage identified, though Abraham traditions need a later cross-batch dossier.
- **3:96:** First sanctuary at Bakkah; independent pre-Islamic attestation and dating require separate historical investigation.
- **3:97:** Abraham's standing-place, sanctuary and pilgrimage; do not conflate later Islamic explanations with independently dated pre-Islamic witnesses.
- **3:98–100:** People-of-the-Book polemic and warning; no precise earlier textual parallel established in this preliminary pass.

## Witnesses, dating, and interpretive alternatives

The Masoretic Hebrew, Greek Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch (where applicable), and Dead Sea Scroll witnesses must be distinguished by passage and survival, not presumed to attest identical wording. The Psalm and Torah passages are older literary traditions, but this preliminary document has not collated specific manuscript variants. Interpretations of Genesis 32 as a food-law origin should be distinguished from alternative interpretations of Qur'an 3:93 and its later tafsir. Sanctuary history requires archaeological and textual source criticism independent of devotional tradition.
