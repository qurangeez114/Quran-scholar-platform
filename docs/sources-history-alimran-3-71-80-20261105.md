# Āl ʿImrān 3:71–80 — textual integrity, trust, covenant and prophetic worship

**Research status:** unpublished preliminary dossier (2026-11-05). Not a public Sources card or a fully verified historical dossier. Related screening: `data/sources/staging/coverage-3-71-to-3-80-20261105.json`. Next: 3:81–90.

## Context and chronology
The Qur'anic passage addresses polemic with some People of the Book, entrusted property, oaths and the authority of prophets. It must not be generalized into a factual description of all Jews or Christians. The Pentateuchal legal materials compared below are part of the Hebrew Bible and have a complex composition/redaction history. The dates of traditions, textual compositions, translations, and extant manuscript witnesses are different historical questions; a specific manuscript date is **not established** in this preliminary dossier. The late-antique Qur'anic passage and ancient Israelite legal traditions occupy different institutional contexts.

## Passage-level screening
- **3:71–74:** Accusations of hiding truth, a described strategy of feigned belief, and divine guidance/grace. No sufficiently specific pre-Islamic textual parallel established in this pass. These are Qur'anic rhetorical claims, not independent evidence of a historical incident.
- **3:75–77:** Deposits, fidelity and oaths offer testable comparison to Pentateuchal law. The strongest of the three candidates is the oath theme in 3:77, but no direct literary dependence is established.
- **3:78:** Speech that misrepresents what belongs to scripture. Do not collapse this into a claim that surviving biblical manuscripts were rewritten: the verse's speech-oriented allegation and historical manuscript variation are distinct evidentiary questions.
- **3:79–80:** A prophet does not demand worship of himself, angels or prophets. No specific earlier passage was established in this bounded pass. Comparative work should examine prophetic authority, Jewish angelology and Christian Christology without presuming doctrinal identity.

## Textual witnesses, variants and scholarly interpretations
Hebrew Bible witnesses and ancient translations (including Septuagint and later reception) require separate evaluation. A stable modern web text is a convenient locator, **not** a dated manuscript witness. No collation of Masoretic, Samaritan or Greek witnesses has been completed. Distinguish (a) similar ethical norms shared across religious traditions, (b) deliberate Qur'anic interscriptural argument, and (c) demonstrable literary transmission. The present evidence supports only preliminary thematic comparison. Further interpretive study should consider whether 3:75–77 addresses a concrete local dispute or a generalized rhetorical pattern; neither reading is established here.
