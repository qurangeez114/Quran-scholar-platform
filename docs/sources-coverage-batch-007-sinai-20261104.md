# Sinai, the golden calf, and wilderness provisions — research dossier (draft)

**Qur'an 2:51–60** · Staged research only · 2026-11-04

## Historical and literary frame
This Qur'anic sequence addresses the Israelites through remembered wilderness episodes. It condenses events distributed across Exodus 16–17, 24 and 32. Narrative order, theological framing, and agency vary. A literary comparison does not establish a chain of direct borrowing.

## Additional verses and cautions
- **2:55–56:** Seeing God, thunderbolt and subsequent revival are not to be equated automatically with the Sinai elders' vision in Exodus 24:9–11 or other death/revival motifs. Investigate narrative identity and later Jewish traditions first.
- **2:58–59:** Entering a settlement, commanded words and punitive consequence require a separate comparison with Numbers/Deuteronomy; none has passed the specific-locator threshold in this pass.
- **2:60:** The Qur'an's *twelve springs* are absent from Exodus 17:1–7. Compare Numbers 20:2–13 and possible twelve-tribe exegetical associations, but do not conflate distinct biblical water episodes.
- **2:54:** The Arabic `fa-uqtulū anfusakum` has interpretive complexity. The supplied English translation's parenthetical expansion must not be silently treated as Arabic text.

## Source history and scholarly limitations
The Pentateuch's textual formation is debated, and composition date cannot be equated with the date of a surviving manuscript. The extant Masoretic tradition, Greek Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch can preserve divergent readings; this draft has **not** collated them. Exodus is presented here through the New American Bible Revised Edition as published by the USCCB (English modern critical translation). A Hebrew source-language check, textual apparatus and scholarly bibliography are required before promotion. These are **narrative parallels**, not verbatim quotations.
