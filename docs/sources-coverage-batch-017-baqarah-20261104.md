# Sources batch 017 — Al-Baqarah 2:151–160
Date: 2026-11-04. **10 first-pass verse records; 2 staged loose comparisons; 0 verified/public source associations; 0 published dossiers.**

## Primary passages checked
1. **Q 2:152 and 1 Samuel 2:30** ([Hebrew and Revised JPS 2023](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Samuel.2.30)). The Hebrew text has the reciprocity of divine honoring those who honor God, within the condemnation of Eli's priestly household. Q 2:152 instead commands remembering God and gratitude and promises divine remembrance. This is a broad rhetorical motif, not identical terminology or a historical episode.
2. **Q 2:155 and Leviticus 26** ([Hebrew and Revised JPS 2023](https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.26)). Leviticus 26:3–13 outlines blessings for covenant obedience, and 26:14–46 penalties for disobedience. Q 2:155 anticipates fear, hunger, and losses as testing and announces good news to the patient. Distinct causal frameworks and genres make this at most a tentative thematic contrast. Read the complete chapter and scholarship before considering any publication.

## Historical scope and chronology
Both Hebrew biblical works preserve earlier Israelite literary traditions. Composition/redaction dates remain debated and are not interchangeable with the dates of extant Masoretic manuscripts, the modern Sefaria digital presentation, or the Revised JPS 2023 translation. These passages are not proof of direct transmission to Muhammad or dependence. Later Islamic accounts of Safa and Marwa must not be retrojected as verified pre-Islamic textual witnesses.
