## Dossier draft: Charity, harvest, and recompense

### Historical and literary context
Qur'an [2:261](https://quran.com/2/261) depicts a grain producing seven ears, each with one hundred grains, as an analogy for the multiplication of reward for spending in God's path. [2 Corinthians 9:6](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2corinthians/9) likewise uses sowing and reaping to reason about generosity. **Concrete similarity:** agricultural multiplication illustrates giving. **Concrete difference:** Paul's text contrasts sparse and generous sowing; it does not contain the Qur'anic seven-by-one-hundred arithmetic. The Corinthians passage is part of a letter concerning a collection, while 2:261 opens a Qur'anic sequence of instructions about charity and motive. The shared metaphor is not evidence of direct dependence.

Qur'an [2:269](https://quran.com/2/269) connects God's gift of wisdom to abundant good. [Proverbs 2:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.2.6) says that wisdom comes from the LORD, with knowledge and understanding proceeding from him. The two texts share divine bestowal of wisdom, but Proverbs 2 is a longer parental-instruction poem and not the Qur'anic verse's specific framing. This remains a **broad theological motif**.

Qur'an [2:270](https://quran.com/2/270) concerns God's knowledge of spending and vows and the absence of helpers for wrongdoers. Proverbs 11:24–25 is a loose generosity motif, not a passage-specific match, and its association has been removed from the staged candidate list. It remains a deferred thematic research lead only.

### Dating and transmission cautions
2 Corinthians is commonly assigned to Paul's first-century CE correspondence; its earliest extant papyrus witnesses and later Greek codices are distinct from the date of composition. Proverbs is a composite Hebrew wisdom collection, with individual sayings and redaction not securely dateable to a single authorial moment; extant Masoretic manuscripts are later than composition, and the Greek Septuagint has its own translation history and variant wording. For the Qur'an, early manuscripts and later reading traditions also require separate witness descriptions. **No manuscript collation was performed in this batch.** The USCCB English and Sefaria reference texts are locator aids, not substitutes for collation of Greek and Hebrew critical editions. No transmission pathway or textual borrowing has been established.
