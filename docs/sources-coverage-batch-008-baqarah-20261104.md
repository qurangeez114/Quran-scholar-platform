**Date:** 2026-11-04. **First-pass research only; no public verified links or deployed dossiers.**

## 2:61 — wilderness food complaints
The Hebrew/JPS text of [Numbers 11:4–6](https://www.sefaria.org/Numbers.11.4-6) describes a complaint about manna and memories of Egyptian fish, cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions and garlic. Qur'an 2:61 recalls a request for different plants/foods, including lentils, and Moses' response. The overlap is more specific than a generic hunger motif but the lists are **not identical**: lentils appear in the Qur'an but not Numbers 11:5. This is a staged narrative comparison, **not** a demonstrated direct literary borrowing or an approved public source card.

## 2:67–70 — bovine ritual comparison, not identity
[Numbers 19:1–10](https://www.sefaria.org/Numbers.19.1-10) is a research lead for comparison with the cow episode, **not yet a source-critically verified match**. The biblical law describes a red heifer, without blemish or yoke, used in a corpse-purification rite. Qur'an 2:67–70 describes an instruction to slaughter a cow and successive questions about age and color; the cow is vividly yellow. **Red versus yellow, legal purification versus a dialogic narrative, and different details** prevent identification of the two as the same episode. The candidate is keyed only to 2:67; verses 2:68–70 point to the shared passage-level candidate without artificially inflating independent link counts. The Qur'anic narrative continues beyond this batch to 2:71–73; follow through before making a dossier.
