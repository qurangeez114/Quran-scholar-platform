## Historical dossier draft: Israelite kingship and water testing

**Narrative setting.** Qur'an 2:246–251 narrates an Israelite request for a king, Talut's appointment, the ark as sign, a water test, and a conflict with Jalut. These episodes overlap themes distributed across 1 Samuel 8–10 and 17 and Judges 7. The Qur'anic narrative does not simply reproduce the biblical sequence: Gideon's test is relocated to Talut's troops in the Qur'anic account, and the ark is framed as a sign of kingship.

| Ayah | Earlier primary passage | Similarity | Difference / qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2:246 | [1 Samuel 8:4–22](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1samuel/8) | Israelites ask a prophet for a king to lead battle | Samuel criticizes monarchy; Qur'an emphasizes the request and subsequent reluctance to fight |
| 2:247 | [1 Samuel 9–10](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Samuel.10.17-27) | Saul/Talut selected for kingship | Different signs and objections; wealth/knowledge/physique argument distinctive in Qur'an |
| 2:249 | [Judges 7:4–7](https://www.sefaria.org/Judges.7.4-7) | Water behavior distinguishes soldiers before battle | Gideon rather than Saul; different criteria and circumstances |

**Contextual but not verified as individual links:** Q2:248 should be compared carefully with the ark traditions of 1 Samuel 4–6, and Q2:250–251 with 1 Samuel 17. These are not promoted as new public records here. Q2:241–245 have no specific earlier parallel established in this bounded pass; that is not a claim that no earlier parallels exist.

**Textual witnesses and chronology.** The Hebrew Samuel/Judges narratives belong to composite ancient Israelite textual traditions, with debated composition/redaction chronology. Surviving Masoretic codices are medieval; Septuagint Greek translations have pre-Christian origins, but major surviving complete codices are later. Qumran Samuel witnesses establish earlier manuscript evidence for portions of Samuel, not automatically for every passage under comparison. The shorter Greek form of 1 Samuel 17 and the Goliath-height variant are relevant to a future dossier, but not independently collated here. Dates of narratives, textual composition, and surviving manuscripts must be recorded separately.

**Interpretive alternatives.** Similar narrative building blocks may reflect shared traditions, independent retellings, oral mediation, or literary engagement; these possibilities require evidence and cannot be ranked solely from superficial similarity. The Gideon/Saul leader distinction and difference in selection criteria are especially significant.
