# Āl ʿImrān 3:61–70: Abrahamic identity and interscriptural dialogue

**Status:** unpublished preliminary historical dossier. Reviewed 2026-11-05. Ten verses screened; three staged comparisons; zero verified/public additions. Next: 3:71–80 after checking other workers.

## Context and chronological background
3:61 addresses mutual imprecation in a dispute about Jesus; 3:62–64 emphasize theological claims and shared worship. 3:65–68 contest retrospective Jewish and Christian classifications of Abraham. 3:69–70 address misleading and rejecting signs. These are distinct arguments; shared passage membership alone does not justify attaching a textual parallel to each verse.

## Witnesses, dating and interpretation
Deuteronomy has a long pre-Islamic Hebrew textual history; distinguish compositional development, literary redaction, and surviving witness dates. Romans is a first-century CE Pauline letter; its modern USCCB English translation is not an ancient manuscript. Consult Hebrew/Greek critical editions, variant witnesses, and scholarly bibliographies before public verification. No direct borrowing or dependence is demonstrated. Qur’an 3:61's mutual-imprecation procedure has no source-specific earlier parallel established in this pass. No apocryphal, Syriac, Zoroastrian or rabbinic text is claimed without a relevant traceable passage.
