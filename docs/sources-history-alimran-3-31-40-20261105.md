# Historical research dossier draft — Āl ʿImrān 3:31–40

**2026-11-05 | unpublished, preliminary | no new verified/public citations**

## Narrative and textual scope

3:31–32 opens with obedience and love of God. 3:33–34 names chosen lineages; 3:35–37 narrates the mother of Mary dedicating her unborn child, Mary's birth, Zechariah's care and provisions; 3:38–40 shifts to Zechariah's prayer and annunciation of Yahya (John). Avoid attributing all details of a long episode to every verse. The Qur'an does not name Mary's mother Anna or her father Joachim in these verses.

## Chronological and textual cautions

The Protoevangelium of James is commonly placed in the second century CE in scholarly discussion; the linked New Advent English translation is a modern presentation, not a manuscript witness or critical Greek edition. The date of composition must be distinguished from surviving Greek manuscript dates, later translations and redactions. Luke is a first-century Christian text in standard scholarly dating, but surviving Greek witnesses are later and variant readings require critical-edition review. The Qur'an's seventh-century context is later than these textual traditions, but temporal priority and motif overlap do **not** demonstrate direct borrowing or a specific transmission route. Do not equate Imran with the biblical Amram without textual and genealogical argument.

## Interpretive alternatives

Possible explanations include shared late-antique narrative traditions, independent reconfiguration of earlier motifs, or more specific literary intertextuality. These are competing analytical possibilities rather than demonstrated dependence. The Qur'an's emphasis on Mary's dedication, God's acceptance, and Yahya's prophetic identity must be compared against the individual literary agendas of the apocryphon and Luke. The dossier is a research draft, **not** a public history article.
