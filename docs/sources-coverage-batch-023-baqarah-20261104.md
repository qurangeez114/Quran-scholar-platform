## Source-critical findings

**Q 2:219 / Proverbs 23:29–35.** [Hebrew text with Revised JPS 2023 translation](https://www.sefaria.org/Proverbs.23.29-35). Proverbs 23:29–35 warns about the suffering and impairment connected with wine, using the Hebrew word `יַיִן`. Q 2:219 addresses `الْخَمْرِ وَالْمَيْسِرِ` (intoxicants/wine and gambling), acknowledging benefits while saying sin/harm is greater. Shared concern about wine is **thematic**, not an identical legal judgment or distinctive verbal parallel. Proverbs does not address gambling in this passage. Neither composition dates nor any specific pre-Islamic manuscript witness have been established by this screening. **Staged only**.

**Q 2:220 / Exodus 22:21–23 (English numbering; Hebrew editions may number the same clauses 22:20–22).** [Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.22.21-23) and [Hebrew with commentary](https://www.chabad.org/library/bible_cdo/aid/9883). Exodus prohibits afflicting widows and orphans, with a warning that God will hear their cry. Q 2:220 answers a question about orphans, says improving their condition is good, and permits shared affairs with safeguards grounded in moral intent. Both protect orphans, but Exodus does not prescribe the same property-management arrangement. This is a **legal-ethical thematic parallel**, not a matching rule. Do not mistake medieval commentaries on the web page for the ancient Exodus passage. **Staged only**.

**Q 2:214 / Deuteronomy 8:2–3** is a research lead rather than a stored candidate: Deuteronomy recounts Israel's wilderness hardships as a divine test; Q 2:214 anticipates believers suffering like earlier communities before divine aid. [Deuteronomy text](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.8.2-3). These have different settings and actors, and no distinctive shared narrative has been established. More primary-source analysis required.
