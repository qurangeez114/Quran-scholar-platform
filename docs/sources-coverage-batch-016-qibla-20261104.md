# Batch 016: Al-Baqarah 2:141–150 — prayer direction and sacred geography
**Date:** 2026-11-04. **Status:** unpublished research draft, not a published Stories/History dossier. **Coverage:** 10 first-pass verses; 2 passage-level candidates; 0 verified/public additions.

## Texts inspected and relationship
1. **1 Kings 8:29–30**, [Hebrew and English](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Kings.8.29-30). Solomon asks God to hear prayer directed toward the Temple. In the Hebrew text, `אֶל־הַמָּקוֹם הַזֶּה` denotes direction toward 'this place'. Q 2:144, 149–150 commands turning toward al-Masjid al-Haram; 2:142–145 discusses opposition to a changed direction. Shared feature: ritual orientation to a sacred location. Crucial difference: Jerusalem Temple vs Sacred Mosque, and no qibla-change episode in Kings. **Label: contextual motif / contrast; staged only.**
2. **Daniel 6:11 in the Hebrew Bible numbering (often 6:10 in English editions)**, [Aramaic and English](https://www.sefaria.org/Daniel.6.11). Daniel's windows face Jerusalem (`נֶגֶד יְרוּשְׁלֶם`) and he prays three times daily despite a royal decree. Shared feature: directed prayer. Difference: the Daniel narrative is not about Muhammad's community or a change to Meccan direction. **Label: contextual practice / contrast; staged only.**

These comparisons apply to the **2:142–150 passage** where relevant; they are not ten invented one-to-one correspondences. Q 2:141,146–147 currently have no specific parallel established in this preliminary pass; that is not an absence proof.

## Historical dossier framework (unpublished)
**Topic:** Prayer direction, Jerusalem and Mecca in the Qur'anic community.

**Background and chronology:** Kings locates temple-oriented prayer in the literary account of Solomon; Daniel is set in a Babylonian/Persian court and depicts Jerusalem-facing prayer. Narrative setting is **not** proof of composition date. The Hebrew/Aramaic Masoretic textual witnesses and modern English translations are later than the settings portrayed. The dating of biblical redaction, textual transmission and specific manuscripts requires separate scholarly sourcing; no witness date is invented here.
