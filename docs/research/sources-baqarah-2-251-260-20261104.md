## Historical dossier draft A: David, Goliath, and kingship

**Primary-text anchors.** [Qur'an 2:251](https://quran.com/2/251) names Dawud as the killer of Jalut, then attributes kingship, wisdom and instruction to God. [1 Samuel 17:48–51](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1samuel/17) gives a longer account: David strikes Goliath with a sling-stone and uses Goliath's sword. [1 Samuel 16](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1samuel/16) already recounts David's anointing; his accession to the throne is narrated later in [2 Samuel 2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2samuel/2) and [2 Samuel 5](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2samuel/5). Thus the Qur'an's concise juxtaposition of victory and divine kingship must not be presented as the same chronology as Samuel.

**Transmission caution.** Samuel's Hebrew textual tradition and its Greek witnesses differ substantially in the David-Goliath cycle. The USCCB notes on 1 Samuel 17:4 explicitly report the Goliath height variant (Masoretic six cubits and a span versus Greek and 4QSam(a) four cubits and a span). This is a *variant in Samuel*, not a Qur'anic textual variant. The shorter Greek form of 1 Samuel 17 and Qumran witnesses need passage-specific collation before a critical publication. Hebrew narrative composition/redaction dates are debated; the medieval Masoretic codices, pre-Christian Greek translation tradition and ancient manuscript fragments must be dated separately. No direct dependence mechanism is established.

**Historical context.** The Samuel account situates the confrontation among Philistine and Israelite forces in the Elah Valley, while Qur'an 2:246–251 integrates a king-selection episode, water trial, prayer, and David's victory into a compact teaching narrative. The water-test material in Judges 7 belongs to Gideon in the biblical narrative; see the prior 2:241–250 draft. A complete public dossier should compare Hebrew and Greek editions, geographic setting, Saul/David chronology, interpretive options and a sourced bibliography, with exact links back to each applicable verse.

## Historical dossier draft B: resurrection demonstrations

[Qur'an 2:259](https://quran.com/2/259) concerns a person and a ruined settlement, death for a hundred years, then restoration. [Qur'an 2:260](https://quran.com/2/260) presents Abraham asking how God revives the dead, followed by instructions involving four birds. These are *different scenes*. [Genesis 15:9–11](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.15.9-11) has Abraham and birds in a covenantal ritual, but no resurrection; any association must be marked a loose contrast, not a direct narrative parallel. [Ezekiel 37](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.37) is a possible resurrection/renewal motif for separate evaluation, not an assigned verified reference to 2:259. Translation of Arabic `faṣurhunna` in 2:260 varies, so translations must be identified explicitly rather than silently resolving the verb.

**Chronology and interpretation.** Genesis and Ezekiel are pre-Islamic Hebrew-scriptural traditions with complicated redaction and manuscript histories. Their textual availability does not establish a pathway into the Qur'an. Possible shared motifs, independent didactic retelling and mediated traditions remain competing hypotheses pending source-specific evidence. The dossier must distinguish the dating of compositions, ancient translations and extant witnesses.
