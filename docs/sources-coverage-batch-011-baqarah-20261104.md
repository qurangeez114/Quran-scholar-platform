## Primary text readings
- **Q 2:92 ↔ Exodus 32:1–6, 19–24** — shared calf worship during Moses' absence; Exodus narrates Aaron's actions, Moses' return and broken tablets, whereas the Qur'anic verse compresses the accusation. [Hebrew and Revised JPS 2023](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.32.1-24). This is an earlier narrative tradition, not evidence of direct textual borrowing.
- **Q 2:93 ↔ Exodus 19:16–20 and 24:7** — mountain, Sinai revelation and covenant. Exodus does **not** say the mountain was lifted over the people, and the responses to covenant obligations differ. [Exodus 19](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.19.16-20), [Exodus 24:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.24.7). Raised-mountain traditions in Jewish interpretation require independently dated witnesses.
- **Q 2:97 ↔ Daniel 8:16 and 9:21** — Gabriel is named as a figure mediating interpretation of visions in Daniel; Qur'an 2:97 identifies Gabriel with revelation to Muhammad. These are distinct episodes and theological contexts. [Daniel 8:16](https://www.sefaria.org/Daniel.8.16), [Daniel 9:21](https://www.sefaria.org/Daniel.9.21).
- **Q 2:98 ↔ Daniel 10:13, 21** — Michael is named as a heavenly prince; Qur'an 2:98 names Michael alongside Gabriel in a polemic about hostility to angels. No shared event is claimed. [Daniel 10:13](https://www.sefaria.org/Daniel.10.13), [Daniel 10:21](https://www.sefaria.org/Daniel.10.21).

The cited Hebrew Bible passages are earlier literary traditions; their redactional histories, earliest extant manuscript witnesses, Masoretic transmission, and modern translation dates must be separately documented. Sefaria's displayed Hebrew and Revised JPS 2023 translations are working textual witnesses, not proof of an identical textual form in seventh-century Arabia.
