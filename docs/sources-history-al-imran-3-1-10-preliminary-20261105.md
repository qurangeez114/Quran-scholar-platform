# Āl ʿImrān 3:1–10 — preliminary Sources ↔ History research dossier

**Status:** unpublished, preliminary; **new verified public links: 0**. This is a research note, **not** a completed historical dossier or evidence of literary borrowing.

## Scope and historical framing
The opening of Sura 3 moves from the mysterious letters (3:1) and divine uniqueness (3:2), through the Qur'an's explicit reference to the Torah and Gospel (3:3–4), to divine knowledge, formation in the womb, interpretive ambiguity, prayer, eschatological gathering, and worldly wealth (3:5–10). These are different kinds of material; a thematic resemblance should not be treated as a specific textual source.

### 3:3–4 — Torah and Gospel
The Qur'an explicitly names the **Tawrāt** and **Injīl**, presenting the revealed Book as confirming what preceded it. The **Torah** denotes an earlier Jewish scriptural tradition; the **Gospel** in Qur'anic discourse is described as given to Jesus, and must not simply be equated without qualification to the four canonical Christian Gospels. This is an **interscriptural claim**, not a demonstrated line-by-line parallel.

**Research questions:** Which understandings of Torah and Gospel were circulating in late antique Arabia? How do Jewish, Greek, Syriac, and other Christian manuscript traditions differ from later printed translations? What do early Qur'anic commentators say about the scope of “confirming”? Avoid assuming any one surviving manuscript was the immediate textual source.

### 3:7 — clear and ambiguous passages
The verse raises interpretation and epistemic limits. A comparison with Jewish and Christian interpretive traditions could be fruitful, but this preliminary pass found **no precisely verified pre-Islamic primary passage** supporting a public verse-level link. Differences between Arabic readings/punctuation and exegetical positions on where the pause occurs must be described before an interpretive conclusion is published.

### 3:8–10 — prayer, gathering and judgment
Prayers for guidance, a final gathering, and the insufficiency of wealth against divine judgment are broad motifs. They are **not** individually matched to earlier sources here. Future candidates need precise book/chapter/verse or manuscript locators, primary text or critical translation, chronology, and substantive differences.

## Transmission and scholarly limitations
The composition dates of ancient scriptural traditions, their manuscript witnesses, and modern printed editions are **different historical quantities**. A late manuscript does not alone imply a late composition; an early textual tradition does not demonstrate that a specific version was available to the Qur'an's audience. Critical witnesses, variants, original-language quotations, and academic bibliography remain **unverified here**. No dependence, copying, or historical contact is asserted.
