**Chronological and textual setting:** Leviticus, Exodus, Deuteronomy and Numbers are parts of the Hebrew Torah with complex pre-Islamic literary and redaction histories; their literary composition is not identical to the date of any surviving Hebrew manuscript. The medieval Masoretic textual tradition is not itself a seventh-century Arabian witness. Ancient versions (Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch, Dead Sea Scrolls where relevant) must be collated before making textual transmission claims. Modern Revised JPS 2023 is a translation, not an ancient witness.

**Comparative analysis:** Dietary rules share a swine prohibition but differ in enumerated prohibitions and emergency exception. Retaliation passages differ in legal scenario and compensation. Inheritance and bequest are distinct legal mechanisms. Jewish halakhic and late-antique Christian receptions may offer historical context, but none is assumed to be a Qur'anic source without date-specific evidence.

**Interpretive alternatives:** common regional legal traditions; independent formulations of analogous norms; later interpretive convergence; textual interaction (unproven). No hypothesis of copying or direct dependence is established.
