# An-Nisāʾ 4:91–100 — preliminary legal and migration research (unpublished)

**Status:** first-pass dossier draft only; no Sources cards or verified public source associations. Reviewed 2026-11-06. All ten verse keys are recorded in the companion JSON. Do not count this as comprehensive historical review.

## Historical frame and primary-text comparisons

### 4:92 — accidental homicide and legal remedies
Qur'an 4:92 distinguishes accidental killing and prescribes manumission and compensation, with treaty/community distinctions and a fasting alternative. Numbers 35:9–34 provides cities of refuge, distinguishes intentional homicide, and invokes adjudication and the blood avenger. Deuteronomy 19:1–13 similarly distinguishes inadvertent and intentional killing and regulates refuge. **Shared:** a juridical distinction based on intent. **Differences:** the Israelite refuge-city institutions are not the Qur'anic compensatory/expiatory procedure. This is a legal comparison, not an assertion of copying. English ESV passages were inspected at https://www.esv.org/Nm35.9-34%3BDe4.41-43%3BDe19.1-13%3BJos20/ ; Qur'an 4:92 Arabic and English at https://www.quran.is/english2/4/92 .

### 4:97–100 — migration, responsibility and refuge
Qur'an 4:97 challenges capable persons who did not migrate; 4:98–99 exempts the powerless and anticipates pardon; 4:100 promises refuge and reward to migrants. Exodus 22:21 and Leviticus 19:33–34 regulate **hosts' treatment of resident foreigners**, invoking Israel's experience in Egypt. **Shared:** migrant/stranger language at a broad ethical level. **Difference:** the subjects, obligations, and theological rationales differ; these may belong only as comparative background rather than verse-level Sources cards. English ESV read at https://www.esv.org/Exodus%2B22%3A21%3BExodus%2B23%3A9%3BLeviticus%2B19%3A33%E2%80%9334/ . Do not attach this motif indiscriminately to 4:98–99.

## Dating, versions, transmission and alternative readings
The Hebrew Bible's legal corpora were composed and edited before Islam in forms whose dating is debated; their modern English translations are **not** ancient witnesses. Hebrew Masoretic textual witnesses, Greek Septuagint variants, Samaritan Pentateuch evidence where relevant, and critical apparatus have **not** been collated for this dossier. Thus neither precise variant claims nor manuscript-based dependence arguments can yet be made. Interpretations may view these passages as distinct legal traditions sharing broader ancient Near Eastern concerns, or as independent prescriptions addressing different social structures. Further scholarly bibliography is required before public release.
