## Narrative setting and chronological background
Q 3:11 recalls Pharaoh and earlier rejecters of signs, without retelling a complete episode. The Egyptian confrontation and sea deliverance in Exodus 7–14 belong to the biblical literary tradition. The literary composition, redaction, and surviving manuscripts of Exodus are distinct questions; no precise dates or causal transmission claims are assigned here.

Q 3:13 refers to two opposing groups and the perception of their numbers. It should first be read within Qur'anic context and later Islamic interpretations of Badr. No particular pre-Islamic battle passage is sufficiently specific to list as an external parallel in this pass.

Q 3:18 is a doctrinal statement of divine unity and testimony. The Shema in Deuteronomy 6:4 is a potentially relevant *doctrinal comparison*, not a narrative source or evidence of copying.
