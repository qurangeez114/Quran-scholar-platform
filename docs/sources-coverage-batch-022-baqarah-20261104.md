## Source-critical evidence

Qur'an 2:210 asks whether they await God coming in shades of clouds, with angels, and the matter decided. [Exodus 19:9–11](https://mechon-mamre.org/p/pt/pt0219.htm) in the Hebrew-English text describes YHWH coming to Moses in a thick cloud, with a planned Sinai descent. The cloud manifestation is shared imagery; the location, narrative sequence, purpose and angelic element differ. Hebrew phrase עַב הֶעָנָן is a thick cloud, not a claim of identical wording with Qur'anic ظُلَلٍ مِّنَ الْغَمَامِ. This is an analogy, not an established source.

[Daniel 7:13–14](https://www.sefaria.org/Daniel.7.13-14) in the Aramaic text depicts one *like a human being* arriving with heaven's clouds to the Ancient of Days and receiving kingship. This differs from the Qur'anic divine coming and angels. It must not be misrepresented as the same subject or event. These are two distinct candidate comparisons, not two verified associations.

## Chronology and transmission limitations

Exodus is part of the ancient Hebrew Pentateuch and Daniel 7 of an Aramaic apocalyptic vision preserved in the Hebrew Bible. Composition and redaction chronologies are debated; the modern web editions and translations consulted are not themselves pre-Islamic manuscript witnesses. Assess Masoretic, Septuagint, and relevant Dead Sea Scroll evidence by specific verse and edition before publication. A shared cloud motif cannot by itself demonstrate literary dependence. Compare Jewish and Christian reception only with securely dated, traceable primary passages.
