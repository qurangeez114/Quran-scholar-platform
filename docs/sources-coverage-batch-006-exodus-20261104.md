## Primary-text comparison candidates

### 2:49 — Pharaoh's oppression / Exodus 1:11–22

The Qur'an recounts the Israelites' rescue from Pharaoh's people, including harsh oppression, killing of sons and preservation of daughters. Exodus 1:11–22 also narrates forced labor and an order targeting Hebrew boys while sparing girls. **Differences:** Exodus explicitly includes the midwives Shiphrah and Puah, their resistance to the initial order, and a later order to throw boys into the Nile; these details are absent from Q 2:49. [Qur'an 2:49](https://quran.com/al-baqarah/49); [Exodus 1:15–22, ESV](https://www.esv.org/Ex1.15-22/); [Hebrew source locator](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.1.15-22).

### 2:50 — Sea rescue / Exodus 14:21–31

Both narratives depict a divided sea, Israelite deliverance and the drowning of Egyptian pursuers. **Differences:** Exodus names Moses' gesture, an east wind, dry ground and the chariots, while Q 2:50 is a compact divine reminder emphasizing rescue and the audience's witnessing. [Qur'an 2:50](https://quran.com/en/al-baqarah/49-53); [Exodus 14:21–31, ESV](https://www.esv.org/Exodus+14:21-31/); [Hebrew source locator](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.14.21-31).

Both are **specific narrative parallels** rather than merely loose motifs, but neither proves direct literary borrowing. Both are **staged** pending Hebrew philology, critical text review and source-history analysis. A later editor must distinguish the composition and redaction of Exodus traditions from the dates of extant manuscript witnesses.

## Historical dossier plan: Exodus and Qur'anic retelling

A full dossier should distinguish (a) Exodus 1's setting of labor and decrees, (b) Exodus 2's Moses infancy narrative, (c) Exodus 12–14's departure and crossing, (d) Exodus 15's song, (e) Qur'an 2:49–50's retrospective appeal, and (f) further Qur'anic Exodus passages such as 7:103–137, 10:75–93, 20:9–80, 26:10–68 and 28:3–46. Compare different narrative sequence, emphasis and theological rhetoric without presuming that either retelling copies the other. The canonical Hebrew Exodus text predates Islam as a tradition, but the composition and dating of layers are debated. The Hebrew Masoretic text, Greek Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch and relevant Qumran witnesses require edition-specific comparison; do not imply all witnesses preserve identical readings or that a modern edition's publication date is an ancient manuscript date. Consult peer-reviewed historical scholarship before making claims about an actual Egyptian pharaoh or archaeological corroboration.
