## Context and chronology

This passage addresses the early Muslim community's religious fidelity, solidarity and ethical responsibility, then eschatological accountability and communal identity. Its Qur'anic formulation belongs to the seventh-century Arabian setting. Paul's First Letter to the Corinthians is conventionally situated in the mid-first century CE, and 1 Peter is an early Christian text with disputed authorship/composition dating. **These approximate literary composition dates are not dates of the surviving manuscripts**. Neither similarity below establishes a route of transmission or borrowing.

## Verse-by-verse screening

- **3:101:** Revelation and messenger, holding fast to God; no specific earlier passage established in this pass.
- **3:102:** Fear/reverence of God and submission at death; no precise passage established.
- **3:103:** Unity and reconciliation after hostility; compare 1 Cor 1:10 cautiously.
- **3:104:** Call to good and prohibition of wrong; requires dedicated Jewish and Christian legal-ethical research, not an automatic link to generic morality.
- **3:105:** Avoid division after proofs; 1 Cor 1:10 only a broad motif.
- **3:106–107:** Contrasting faces at judgment and mercy; imagery needs a wider, source-specific eschatological comparison before any external attribution.
- **3:108:** Divine recitation and justice; no precise earlier source established.
- **3:109:** Divine ownership of heavens and earth; common monotheistic theme, no distinctive source assigned.
- **3:110:** Exemplary community and moral obligations; 1 Peter 2:9–10 is a comparative contrast, not an identification of the same group.

## Variants, transmission and scholarly alternatives

Potential interpretive approaches include shared late-antique religious-ethical vocabulary, independent appeals to communal solidarity, or interaction with scriptural discourses. The current evidence does **not** discriminate between these possibilities. Any stronger historical hypothesis needs dated witnesses, distinctive phraseology and a plausible transmission setting. Greek manuscript variants and Syriac reception have not been collated. No invented early witness or dependence claim is included.
