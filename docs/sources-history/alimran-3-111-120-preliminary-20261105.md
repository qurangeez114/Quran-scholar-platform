## Scope and chronology
This passage contrasts the faithful and unfaithful, describes upright People of the Book (3:113–115), uses a destructive wind simile (3:117), and warns against harmful confidants (3:118–120). The Qur'anic setting is seventh-century Arabia; the Psalter and Proverbs are composite pre-Islamic biblical collections with long editorial histories. Do not equate composition dates with dates of surviving Hebrew manuscripts, and do not assume the medieval Masoretic manuscript witness is the date of composition.

## Passage-level distinctions
3:111–112 concern opposition, humiliation and covenant language, not merely generic persecution. 3:113–115 explicitly distinguish upright People of the Book, so the dossier must not portray the entire group as uniformly condemned. 3:116–117 contrast material security with divine judgment and employ a wind that destroys cultivation; no exact earlier text has been checked here. 3:118–120 concerns trust, hidden hostility and patient response, with polemical rhetoric that must be contextualized rather than universalized to modern interfaith relations.

## Textual transmission and interpretation
The English USCCB translations were inspected for the two candidate passages. Neither the Hebrew of the Masoretic tradition, Septuagint Greek variants, nor critical apparatus has been collated. The passages are independent literary genres: Hebrew wisdom/poetry versus Qur'anic communal exhortation. Similar ethical vocabulary can reflect common traditions without demonstrating textual borrowing. Future work should consider alternative interpretations and precise historical referents; do not infer a transmission route from motif alone.
