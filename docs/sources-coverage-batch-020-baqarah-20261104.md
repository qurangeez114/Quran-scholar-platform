### Fasting and self-denial: Q2:183–187
1. **Q2:183 ↔ Leviticus 16:29–31** ([ESV text](https://www.esv.org/Lv16.29-31%3BLv23.26-29%3BNm29.7/)): earlier Israelite calendrical self-denial and cessation of work at the Day of Atonement. The Qur'an describes prescribed fasting, then identifies Ramadan in 2:185, and gives exemptions and compensation in 2:184–185. **Not an identical observance or exact quotation.**
2. **Q2:183 ↔ Isaiah 58:3–7** ([ESV text](https://www.bible.com/bible/59/ISA.58.3-7)): fasting criticized when coupled with oppression; just treatment and aid to the poor emphasized. The Qur'an emphasizes the prescription and aim of fasting. **Broad ethical motif, not a shared narrative.**
3. **Q2:184–187:** specific provisions concerning illness, travel, compensatory feeding, Ramadan, marital relations, dawn boundary and mosque retreat require individual historical examination; no fabricated earlier legal equivalence is assigned.

### Fighting and its limits: Q2:190
**Q2:190 ↔ Deuteronomy 20:10–18** ([ESV text](https://www.esv.org/verses/Deuteronomy%2B20%3A10%E2%80%9318/)): both regulate warfare, but Deuteronomy distinguishes distant cities from conquest towns and specifies siege, servitude, plunder and destruction. Q2:190 commands fighting those who fight the believers and forbids transgression. **A legal contrast only**, not an equivalent command or proof of dependence. Read Q2:190 with 2:191–193 and document divergent tafsir interpretations in the fuller dossier.
