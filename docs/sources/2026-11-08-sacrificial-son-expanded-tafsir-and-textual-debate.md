# Abraham's sacrificial son — sourced tafsīr debate and textual argument research

**Last review:** 2026-11-08  
**Focus:** Qurʾān 37:101–113; supplementary cross-reference verses 11:71, 15:53, 51:28.  
**User-submitted lead:** Dr. Yasir Qadhi, *Library Chats E7: Ismāʿīl vs. Isḥāq As the Sacrifice: A Case Study of Tafsīr and Scholarly Influence* ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CANtmlGngiw), originally published **2020-09-25**). The official YouTube video metadata was retrieved but its captions/transcript were **unavailable**; specific attributions to the speaker in user-provided summaries have not been independently checked against a complete transcript.

## Distinguish the historical strata

1. **The Qurʾān itself.** Qurʾān 37:101 announces an unnamed *ghulām ḥalīm*, 37:102–111 describes the attempted sacrifice and ransom of an unnamed son, and 37:112 names Isaac with the news of his prophetic future. Two other announcements use *ghulām ʿalīm* at **15:53** and **51:28** (NOT 51:53–54); Isaac is named in the promise to Sarah at **11:71**. The son's name is **absent** from 37:102–107.
2. **Earlier textual witnesses.** Hebrew Bible Genesis 22 and Jubilees 17–18 explicitly name Isaac; Qumran 4Q225 names Isaac in fragmentary text with lacunae and restoration. These are `ancient_sources` and `qh_verified_verse_story_sources` records, not statements about what early Muslim scholars preferred.
3. **Classical exegetical opinions.** The `classical_tafsir_positions` table records dated interpretations, exact Qurʾānic verse links, the commentator's source work and URL, and the distinction between what the author **argues** and what the author **transmits**. A person quoted in a commentary did not necessarily hold that view, even when a source names them.
4. **Modern scholarly lecture.** The Qadhi video is a research lead and contemporary interpretation of tafsīr history. Until its full spoken wording is obtained, it cannot be cited as proof that specific names/figures or counts were actually mentioned in the video.

## Interpretive arguments displayed in Stories

- **Sequence (37:101–112).** Later mention of Isaac after the ransom is read as a *second birth announcement* in an Ishmael reading, and as *subsequent prophecy announcement* in an Isaac reading. **Neither interpretation puts a name in 37:102–107.**
- **Terminology.** `ghulām ḥalīm` at **37:101**, `ghulām ʿalīm` at **15:53 and 51:28**. The contrast is an exegetical argument, not a grammatical identity marker.
- **Other texts.** At 11:71 Isaac and Jacob are named in Sarah's annunciation. The nature and timing of this promise is argued differently across tafsīr works.
- **Geography.** Mina, Makkah, and reports of the ram's horns are features of Muslim ritual history and later narration; they do not automatically prove where Abraham's episode occurred.
- **Comparison.** The claim that Jewish or Christian accounts influenced an early Muslim transmitter must be assessed report by report. Genesis 22 explicitly says Isaac; the Qurʾān itself does not name the sacrificed son.

## Unverified reader-submitted leads

Do not create separate `classical_tafsir_positions` entries attributing firm personal views to ʿUmar, ʿAlī, Ibn Masʿūd, Ibn ʿAbbās, Jābir, Ibn ʿUmar, Mālik, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Mujāhid, ʿIkrimah or Saʿīd ibn Jubayr merely because they are named in a later source. Track the *exact transmitter chain, classical compilation, edition, variant attributions and grading*. Ibn ʿAbbās is explicitly cited in **both** camps.

The submitted quantitative assertions “at least eight Companions”, “about fifteen Successors”, “the undisputed early majority” and “a later complete scholarly consensus” are **not yet independently verified statistics** and are not presented as historical findings. A qualitative description of competing generations is justified, but the tally requires deduplication and transmission scrutiny.

Additional promising works for a later verified batch: al-Azraqī's *Akhbār Makkah* (including opposed traditions), al-Wāḥidī's tafsīr, al-Thaʿlabī's tafsīr and independent academic histories of sacrificial-son identification. Do not import these as source-verified personal opinions without reviewing original passages.
