## Context, chronology and manuscript caveats
Genesis is part of the Hebrew Torah/Pentateuch, and the literary traditions preserved in Genesis predate the Qur'an, but compositional history and precise dates are debated. The Sefaria Hebrew edition is an accessible witness to the received Masoretic text, **not a manuscript from the period of composition**. Differences between ancient Hebrew witnesses and translations (e.g. Septuagint, Syriac Peshitta, Targums) remain to be investigated. The Qur'an belongs to a seventh-century Arabian context. Similarities may reflect wider narrative circulation, interpretive traditions, or shared scriptural heritage; they alone do not establish a direct textual borrowing path.

## Interpretive alternatives and pending questions
- What does Qur'anic **al-asmāʾ kullahā** ('all the names') encompass? Do not assume the Qur'an explicitly limits it to animal names as Genesis does.
- What role does the Qur'anic angelic test play compared with the Genesis account of Adam naming creatures? Explore demonstrably early Jewish traditions, with source-specific dating, rather than relying on much later medieval commentaries as pre-Islamic evidence.
- The Qur'an identifies Satan/Iblis in this cycle; Genesis 3's serpent must not be automatically labeled 'Satan' as though Genesis itself says so.
- Distinguish garden location, human culpability, repentance, divine mercy, and future guidance in each text.
