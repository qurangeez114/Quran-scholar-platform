**Screened:** 2026-11-06. **Scope:** preliminary verse-by-verse research, not comprehensive. **Public status:** no source cards or dossiers promoted. Checkpoint: `data/sources/staging/screening-batch-063-nisa-4-121-130-20261106.json`.

## Literary and legal context
4:121–124 juxtaposes eschatological outcomes, claims of privilege and accountability for deeds. 4:125–126 invokes Abraham as a paradigm of upright submission and calls him God's close friend. 4:127–130 turns to orphan women's rights, marital reconciliation, fairness between wives, and separation. These verses do not collectively retell one earlier narrative.

**Important Abraham transmission note:** [Isaiah 41:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Isaiah.41.8), [2 Chronicles 20:7](https://www.sefaria.org/II_Chronicles.20.7), and James 2:23 are relevant to the development of the Abraham-friend designation. Hebrew and Greek wording is not necessarily identical to Arabic `khalīl`; these witnesses must be separately compared before claiming an exact verbal parallel or reconstructing a transmission route. The scriptural texts' composition dates are not dates of surviving manuscript copies. Neither shared title nor theological motif alone proves dependence.

## Dating, witnesses and interpretive limits
James is a Christian Greek-language text transmitted in multiple manuscript witnesses; its precise date of composition, authorial setting and textual variants must be documented with a critical edition. Exodus is an ancient Israelite legal text with a complex compositional and textual history; Masoretic, Greek and other witnesses should not be collapsed into a single undated text. A dated manuscript is evidence of transmission, not necessarily of original composition. No primary-text manuscript collation was completed here.

The biblical orphan-law comparison is a thematic/legal comparison, not an established source of Qur'an 4:127. Marital-law comparisons need their own independent corpus investigation. The absence of an identified parallel in a preliminary pass does not mean none exists.
