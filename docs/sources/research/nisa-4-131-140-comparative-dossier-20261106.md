# An-Nisāʾ 4:131–140: preliminary comparative dossier (unpublished)

**Status:** preliminary, not a verified/public source association. Reviewed 2026-11-06. Ten unique verses are in the accompanying checkpoint; no database writes or claims of textual dependence.

## Context and sequence
4:131–134 discusses divine sovereignty, previous scriptural communities, and recompense; 4:135 commands justice and truthful testimony, even against oneself or close relations; 4:136–140 moves to faith, hypocrisy, and avoiding gatherings mocking divine signs. The passage is not a single story and must not be assigned a blanket historical parallel.

## Candidate comparison: 4:135 and Leviticus 19:15
| Aspect | Qur’an 4:135 | Leviticus 19:15 |
|---|---|---|
| Shared norm | Stand firmly for justice, including when inconvenient | Render fair judgment without favoritism to poor or great |
| Distinct emphasis | Witness against oneself, parents or relatives; rich or poor; do not distort testimony | Judicial impartiality toward the poor and socially powerful, and fair judgment of a neighbor |
| Relationship | Ethical/legal thematic correspondence | Not a shared narrative or evidence of literary borrowing |

**Primary source inspected:** Hebrew Leviticus 19:15 and JPS 1917/1985 English translations displayed at https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.19.15 . The Hebrew reads in part `לֹא־תַעֲשׂוּ עָוֶל בַּמִּשְׁפָּט` and `בְּצֶדֶק תִּשְׁפֹּט עֲמִיתֶךָ` (do not do injustice in judgment; judge your fellow fairly). Edition-specific differences and translation rights must be respected. The modern digitized Masoretic text is not itself an ancient manuscript witness.

**Dating and transmission:** Leviticus belongs to the Hebrew Torah, whose textual formation is debated and precedes Islam; this does not date any surviving codex to the time of composition. No Dead Sea Scrolls, Samaritan Pentateuch, Septuagint, or medieval Masoretic manuscript collation was performed here. Do not assign an unsupported exact composition date or claim manuscript identity.

**Interpretations and limitations:** Both texts advocate impartial justice, a widespread legal and moral norm. The Qur’anic demand for testimony against self and kin is not expressed in the same way in this Leviticus verse. Further work should test Exodus 23:2–3 and Deuteronomy 16:19 and examine late-antique Jewish and Christian ethical discourse before any stronger classification.
