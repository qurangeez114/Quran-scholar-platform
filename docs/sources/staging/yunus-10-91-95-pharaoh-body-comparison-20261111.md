# Yunus 10:91–95 — unpublished historical research note

**Status:** preliminary; NOT a verified/public Stories/History dossier. Screened 2026-11-11. No production changes.

## Historical/narrative sequence
- **10:91:** Pharaoh's last-minute profession (10:90) is rebuked as too late. Exodus 14 does not narrate such a profession.
- **10:92:** Pharaoh's body becomes a sign for later people. Exodus 14:30 says Israel saw *Egyptians* dead at the seashore; it does not identify Pharaoh's corpse, describe its preservation, or establish a particular modern mummy.
- **10:93:** Settlement and provisions for the Israelites, followed by disputes. Requires targeted comparisons to wilderness and settlement traditions; none has been checked closely enough for a verse-specific source association.
- **10:94:** Appeal to people who read earlier scripture; a statement about scriptural authority, not itself evidence for any one literary source.
- **10:95:** Warning not to reject divine signs; no specific earlier parallel established in this first pass.

The Qur'anic Arabic فَٱلْيَوْمَ نُنَجِّيكَ بِبَدَنِكَ has a different focus. English renderings of the Qur'anic phrase vary; do not flatten “save/deliver you in your body” into a proven archaeological claim.

## Transmission and dating limits
Exodus belongs to the Hebrew Bible, a textual tradition predating Islam; dating the composition and redaction of the narrative is distinct from dating extant witnesses (Masoretic codices, Septuagint witnesses, Dead Sea Scroll fragments). No critical apparatus, witness-level variant collation, or securely dated manuscript has been reviewed in this batch. The Qur'anic verse is attested in the seventh-century Islamic textual tradition, but no Qur'an manuscript witness has been collated here. Consequently, neither direct dependence nor a named Pharaoh/mummy identification is asserted.

## Interpretation and unresolved research
Evaluate early tafsir readings of **bi-badanika**, compare the Septuagint and Samaritan Pentateuch readings at Exodus 14:30, review scholarly discussions of the sea narrative and Pharaoh's fate, and verify production duplicates before considering any public association. Avoid conflating the biblical collective death scene with the Qur'anic individual body sign.
