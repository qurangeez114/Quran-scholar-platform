# Yunus 10:86–90 — unpublished comparative research notes

**Status:** Preliminary; not a verified/public historical dossier. **Reviewed:** 2026-11-11. **Next:** 10:91–95. No production writes.

## Passage map
- 10:86: prayer for rescue from an unbelieving people; no specific earlier textual association established in this pass.
- 10:87: Moses and Aaron receive instructions for Israelite houses in Egypt, orientation and prayer; research needed into Exodus 12 and relevant Jewish traditions before asserting a relationship.
- 10:88: Moses' prayer concerning Pharaoh's wealth and the hardening of hearts; compare carefully with Exodus 7–14, distinguishing divine hardening, Pharaoh's own actions, and a prayer by Moses.
- 10:89: answer to Moses and Aaron's prayer; no exact earlier locator verified.
- 10:90: crossing, pursuit and Pharaoh's confession at the point of drowning; **unverified candidate:** Exodus 14:21–28.

## Primary text inspected for 10:90
- Qur'an 10:90, Arabic and English: https://legacy.quran.com/10/1-90
- Exodus 14:21–28, King James Version: https://www.bible.com/bible/1/EXO.14.21-28.KJV

**Similarity:** Israel escapes through the sea and the Egyptian forces pursue; divine deliverance and drowning frame both narratives.

**Difference:** Exodus 14 details an east wind, dry ground, the pillar of cloud/fire, chariots and the destruction of Pharaoh's pursuing army; it does not narrate Pharaoh personally confessing monotheistic faith as he drowns. Qur'an 10:90 foregrounds that confession. The Exodus passage's reference to Pharaoh's army must not be misrepresented as an explicit description of Pharaoh's own death.

**Relationship:** Shared narrative episode, not demonstrated direct borrowing or textual dependence. The two accounts have distinct narrative emphases.

## Historical/source-critical limits
The Hebrew Bible's compositional history is disputed and cannot be equated with the date of any surviving manuscript. The online KJV text is an early modern English translation, not a critical Hebrew edition. No Hebrew apparatus, Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch, Dead Sea Scroll witness, Syriac Peshitta or relevant Jewish retelling has yet been collated for this dossier. No confident dating or direction-of-dependence claim follows from this preliminary comparison.
