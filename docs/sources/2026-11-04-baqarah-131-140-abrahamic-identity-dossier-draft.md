# Historical dossier draft — Abrahamic descent, Jacob's farewell, and confessional identity (Q 2:131–140)

**Status:** research-only; not published as a verified historical dossier. **Batch 015**, 2026-11-04. Ten individually keyed verses; three staged source comparisons; zero public source promotions.

## Historical and literary setting
The Qur'anic sequence (2:131–140) moves from Abraham's submission and instruction to his descendants, to Jacob's deathbed profession, then to confessional disagreements concerning Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and the tribes. It should be compared to distinct episodes in Genesis, not flattened into one narrative. Genesis contains Abrahamic covenant traditions, the Isaac–Ishmael distinction, Jacob's birth narrative, and Jacob's farewell to his sons. These literary settings are not identical to the Qur'anic argument about religious identity.

## Primary-text reading and version control
The linked Sefaria passages expose the Hebrew Masoretic textual tradition with modern translation options. For Genesis 17:18–21, the Hebrew names יִשְׁמָעֵאל (Ishmael) and יִצְחָק (Isaac), and 17:21 identifies Isaac as the covenant recipient, whereas 17:20 explicitly blesses Ishmael. Genesis 25:26 names יַעֲקֹב (Jacob). These are passage-level observations, not a claim that one textual recension was accessible to the Qur'anic milieu. Before publication, record exact edition/translation versions, compare Septuagint and Peshitta variants where relevant, and assess textual-critical notes.

## Chronology, manuscripts and limitations
Genesis' composition/redaction is historically debated; its narrative setting in patriarchal antiquity is not a composition date. The Masoretic Hebrew text presented in a modern digital edition is not itself an ancient manuscript witness. Greek Septuagint and Syriac Peshitta transmission histories need separate dating and variant assessment. Likewise, later rabbinic or Christian interpretations cannot automatically be cited as securely pre-Islamic without textual and witness chronology. No literary borrowing is inferred from these thematic or genealogical overlaps.

## Scholarly interpretations to investigate
