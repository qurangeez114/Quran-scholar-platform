# An-Nisāʾ 4:21–30: kinship, marriage, property — unpublished comparative dossier

**Review status:** preliminary (2026-11-05). All comparisons staged, none verified for public Sources cards. Primary passages below were consulted in English translation except Leviticus 19, which remains an unchecked lead. No Hebrew critical edition or manuscript collation completed.

## Qur'anic sequence and historical questions

4:21 condemns taking back marital property after a solemn bond. 4:22 forbids marriage to women previously married to one's father. 4:23 specifies prohibited kinship marriages, including nursing relations, step-relations, and simultaneous sisters. 4:24–25 address married women, captive/enslaved women, bridal payment and social status. 4:26–28 offer theological framing; 4:29–30 prohibit wrongful appropriation and killing, with divine judgment. Treat these as distinct legal topics rather than one undifferentiated parallel.

| 4:23 | Leviticus 18:6–18 | Numerous prohibited relatives, including sisters, aunts, daughters-in-law and two sisters concurrently | Qur'an includes milk kinship and its own stepdaughter conditions; the lists are not identical | English text inspected; unverified |
| 4:29 | Leviticus 19:11,13 | Prohibition on dishonest appropriation | Qur'an specifically refers to trade by mutual consent and to killing | Locator staged, direct text not checked |

The relation for 4:22–23 is stronger than a generic motif because both traditions contain concrete overlapping kinship restrictions. That is **not** proof of direct textual borrowing. Do not imply all restrictions originated in one source, and do not treat later rabbinic commentary as pre-Islamic without work-specific dating.

## Composition and manuscript cautions

The Pentateuchal laws are pre-Islamic textual traditions; their composition is composite and debated. A dated translation or online edition is **not** a dated manuscript witness.
