# Āl ʿImrān 3:161–170 — unpublished research draft

**Status:** Preliminary, unverified. Not suitable for public Sources cards.

## Historical context and verse-level scope

Verses 161–168 discuss misappropriation, responsibility, the messenger's teaching role, and responses to military setbacks. They are embedded in an early Islamic context, not accounts that can be matched as historical events to pre-Islamic literature. Verses 169–170 shift to the status of those killed in God's path and their joy. The comparison below applies to 169–170 only.

## Staged source table

| 3:169–170 | 2 Maccabees 7:9,14 | Martyrdom motif / theological contrast | Faithful deaths are framed by expectation of divine vindication and life | The Maccabean martyrs emphasize bodily resurrection and judgment in a persecution narrative; Qur'an 3:169–170 emphasizes current life, provision and rejoicing |

## Chronology and textual transmission

Both works belong to pre-Islamic Jewish Greek textual traditions, but their dates of composition, earliest surviving Greek witnesses, manuscript recensions and translation-specific choices have not been independently established in this batch. Work composition date must not be confused with dates of surviving manuscripts. No original-language critical collation has been done; do not promote these leads.

## Interpretive cautions

Shared religious expectations about divine vindication are not evidence of direct borrowing. Other explanations include common late antique religious discourse, parallel doctrinal development, and broad theological resemblance. The two source passages do not narrate the battle behind the Qur'anic context.
