# Sources ↔ History dossier draft: contracts, witnesses, pledges and responsibility (Q 2:281–286)

**Status:** unpublished, preliminary screening (2026-11-05). Not a verified Sources card. This is a bounded research note, not a comprehensive dossier.

## Context and comparison

Q 2:281 frames the debt regulations with divine reckoning. Q 2:282 prescribes documenting deferred obligations with a scribe and witnesses, including a gender-specific witness arrangement; Deuteronomy 19:15 is concerned with establishing testimony in accusations rather than debt contracts. The similarity is a broad evidentiary principle, not an identical law.

Q 2:283 provides for a pledge when traveling without a scribe and requires trustworthy discharge of entrusted obligations. Deuteronomy 24:10–13 concerns a creditor's collection of a pledge and protection of a poor debtor's garment. It does not provide the Qur'anic documentation rule. Q 2:284–285 moves from inward accountability to affirmation of revelation and messengers. No source-specific earlier passage was established in this short screening; this does not mean none exists.

Q 2:286 says God does not charge a soul beyond its capacity and closes with petitions for mercy. 1 Corinthians 10:13 discusses God's limiting temptation and providing endurance; the subject differs from imposed religious responsibility and the Qur'anic prayer.

## Dating and transmission cautions

Deuteronomy's literary formation and redaction predate Islam, but individual passages have complex compositional histories; the medieval Masoretic manuscripts are not identical in date to the composition of the law. The Greek Septuagint and other textual witnesses require explicit comparison. 1 Corinthians is conventionally dated to the mid-first century CE; surviving manuscripts are later than its composition. No manuscript collation or exact Greek/Hebrew edition-level verification was completed in this batch. Do not infer dependence from thematic proximity.
