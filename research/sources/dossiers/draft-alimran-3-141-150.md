# Āl ʿImrān 3:141–150 — unpublished comparative research dossier

**Status:** preliminary, not publicly verified (2026-11-05). This draft must not populate public Sources cards.

## Historical and literary setting
The passage continues the discussion of the early Muslim community's experience of battle, trial, mortality, and divine support. Verse 3:144 invokes the mortality of Muhammad and previous messengers, while 3:145 speaks of death by divine permission at an appointed time. Verse 3:146 describes prophets accompanied by many devoted followers who endure suffering. Do not read these as accounts of pre-Islamic battles identical to the early Islamic conflicts.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3:145 | Ecclesiastes 3:2 | loose motif | Birth and death occur in their respective times | Ecclesiastes offers poetic reflection on human seasons, not an explicit decree that no one dies except by God's permission |
| 3:146 | Hebrews 11:32–38 | loose motif | Faithful figures endure warfare, violence and persecution | Hebrews is a Christian retrospective catalogue of exemplary figures, not the same narrated battles or a specific parallel to the Arabic verse |

These are leads only. The Hebrew and Greek originals, editions and early manuscript witnesses have not been collated. A similar theme does not establish textual dependence. The comparative link to Hebrews must not be misrepresented as an account composed before the Hebrew Bible's events: it is a first-century CE Christian interpretation of earlier figures. Manuscript survival dates must be recorded separately from composition estimates.

## Verse-specific no-parallel / further-research decisions
3:141–144 and 3:147–150 have no sufficiently specific primary-text parallel established in this screening. This is **not** a claim that no earlier analogue exists. In particular, 3:144's mention of previous messengers dying is a broad historical premise; the death of Moses (Deuteronomy 34:5) is not automatically a direct textual parallel.
