# Noah's disputed authority and impending judgment — unpublished comparative dossier

**Scope:** Qur'an 11:31–35, contextual continuation of 11:25–30. **Reviewed:** 2026-11-12. **Status:** preliminary, hidden; not a public Stories/History dossier.

## Primary-text access and comparison
- Qur'an 11:31–35, Arabic and English: https://www.quran.is/11 . Qur'anic discourse is seventh-century; this check does not establish the date of any individual manuscript witness.
- Genesis 6:5–13 and 6–9, Hebrew and English displayed by Sefaria: https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.6-9 . This is a received biblical narrative, not a critically collated set of witnesses. Its formation/redaction and dates of extant manuscripts are separate questions.

| Qur'an | Genesis comparison | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 11:31 | Genesis 6:8–9 | Noah's claim not to possess divine treasures, unseen knowledge or angelic status, and his defense of followers have no direct equivalent in the checked Genesis narrative. |
| 11:32 | Genesis 6:11–13 | The people's demand for the threatened punishment is not recorded in Genesis. |
| 11:33 | Genesis 6:13 | Shared impending divine judgment, **not** a matching speech: Genesis has God telling Noah of destruction; the Qur'an has Noah tell challengers God alone can bring it. |
| 11:34 | Genesis 6–9 | No specific equivalent established for the statement about advice, divine misguidance and return to God. |
| 11:35 | No verse-specific Genesis counterpart | An apparent change of address to the allegation that Muhammad fabricated the revelation; do not fold this uncritically into Noah's direct dialogue. |

## Historical and interpretive caution
Genesis places Noah's righteousness, divine judgment, ark-building and household preservation at the center. Qur'an 11:25–34 develops extended public disputation, social conflict and prophetic self-description. The common Flood setting is significant, but it is not proof of textual dependence or five independent direct parallels. The relationship of 11:35 to the surrounding Noah episode requires attention to classical exegesis and rhetorical structure.

Genesis is an earlier textual tradition; a precise composition date is debated and **cannot** be inferred from a later surviving manuscript's date. The displayed Hebrew/English is not a substitute for a critical Hebrew edition, Septuagint, Peshitta, Qumran fragments or other witness collation. No uninspected midrashic, apocryphal, Syriac or Zoroastrian parallels are claimed.
