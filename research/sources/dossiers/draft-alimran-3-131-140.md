# Āl ʿImrān 3:131–140 — unpublished comparative dossier draft

**Status:** preliminary research; NOT verified or publicly displayable. Date: 2026-11-05.

## Literary and historical context

Verses 131–136 move from warnings and obedience to repentance, generosity, anger control and forgiveness. Verses 137–140 exhort listeners to consider earlier peoples and persevere despite battlefield losses. The latter passage belongs to Qur'anic discourse about the early Muslim community; it must not be presented as a retelling of an earlier biblical battle.

## Chronology and witness caveats

Proverbs and Psalms belong to pre-Islamic Hebrew scriptural traditions, but a composition date for each verse must not be conflated with the date of a surviving Hebrew manuscript. Sirach was composed in Jewish antiquity and circulated in Hebrew and Greek; its composition, Greek translation and extant manuscript dates are distinct and require edition-specific documentation. This draft does not claim any particular extant witness has been checked.

## Competing explanations and research limits

Shared ethics may reflect widespread Jewish, Christian and broader late antique moral teaching without requiring direct textual dependence. Similarity of repentance language alone does not establish quotation. All three comparisons require inspection of critical text and appropriate translations before verification.
