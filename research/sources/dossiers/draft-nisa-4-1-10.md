# An-Nisāʾ 4:1–10 — comparative historical dossier (unpublished draft)

Status: preliminary screening, 2026-11-05. No source promoted as verified or published. This draft must not be used as a public Sources card.

## Context and scope

4:1 introduces common human origin and kinship obligations. 4:2–6 concern orphan assets, marriage justice, dower, guardianship, and responsible transfer of property. 4:7–10 address inheritance entitlement and protection of vulnerable relatives. These are distinct legal units, not a single narrative lifted from an earlier source.

## Dating and textual transmission

The Pentateuchal works are pre-Islamic textual traditions. Their compositional histories are complex, and manuscript witness dates must not be equated with original composition. Hebrew Masoretic readings, relevant Dead Sea Scroll witnesses (where present), Greek Septuagint differences and specific critical editions remain uncollated. English translations are not substitutes for this step. The Qur'anic Arabic is inspected through the verse text at https://www.search-the-quran.com/4/1-10 ; its English display is Abdullah Yusuf Ali. The Exodus and Deuteronomy comparison was checked against the ESV rendering at https://www.esv.org/Ex22.22-24%3BDe24.17-22%3BAc6.1%3BJm1.27/ . The Genesis and Numbers locators are **unverified leads**, not direct critical-text checks.

## Interpretive limits

Shared ancient ethical concern for orphans does not prove literary transmission. Qur'an 4:3 and 4:4 require independent research into pre-Islamic marriage institutions; no precise candidate is invented here. Compare legal beneficiaries, obligations, penalties, historical audiences and literary genres before judging relationship strength. Possible scholarly models include shared Near Eastern legal culture, mediated scriptural reception and independent ethical development; evidence is presently insufficient to select a specific dependence model.
