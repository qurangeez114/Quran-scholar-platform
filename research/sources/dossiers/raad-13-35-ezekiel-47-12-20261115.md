# Research dossier (unpublished): Q13:35 and Ezekiel 47:12

**Status:** Preliminary, not approved for public Sources/History. Reviewed 2026-11-15. Linked ayah: 13:35. Related contextual verses: 13:31–34 are screened separately and are not assigned this source.

## Textual setting and historical background
Qur'an 13:35 depicts the promised garden for the righteous: rivers flow beneath, provision and shade persist, and the opposing destiny is fire. The verse does not identify an earthly temple or a healing function for leaves. Ezekiel 47:1–12 depicts a stream emerging from the restored sanctuary, nourishing trees whose leaves do not wither and whose fruit does not fail; 47:12 specifically says fresh fruit appears each month and leaves are for healing. The passage belongs to Ezekiel's temple-restoration vision in the Hebrew Bible, conventionally situated in the sixth-century BCE exilic setting. Composition, redaction, and extant witness dates are not interchangeable and have **not** been independently established here.

## Comparison
| Feature | Qur'an 13:35 | Ezekiel 47:12 |
|---|---|---|
| Water | Rivers under the promised garden | Stream from sanctuary nourishing trees |
| Food | Provision persists | Fruit never fails, new fruit each month |
| Shade/leaves | Shade persists | Leaves do not wither and are for healing |
| Setting | Eschatological destiny of righteous contrasted with fire | Temple and land restoration vision |
| Relation | Shared durable-garden imagery | Not an exact narrative or verbal correspondence |

**Original language checked:** Hebrew Masoretic Ezekiel 47:12 as displayed by Sefaria; the passage uses `לֹא־יִבּוֹל עָלֵהוּ וְלֹא־יִתֹּם פִּרְיוֹ` (its leaf will not wither; its fruit will not fail). Qur'an Arabic at 13:35 includes `أُكُلُهَا دَائِمٌ وَظِلُّهَا` (its provision is continuous and its shade). The words and syntactic constructions differ. This supports a meaningful imagery comparison, **not** a copying or direct-dependence claim.

## Transmission and alternative explanations
Ezekiel's Hebrew tradition has a long transmission history; this review did not collate Dead Sea Scroll fragments, Greek Septuagint, Syriac Peshitta, or critical apparatus for 47:12. Later Jewish and Christian paradise traditions may mediate shared imagery, and generic garden imagery is an alternative explanation. Revelation 22:1–2 is an additional relevant Christian textual witness but was **not** critically checked in this run and is not counted as a candidate link. Do not imply its direct relation to the Qur'an without comparison. A separate literature review should assess reception of Ezekiel's vision and Qur'anic garden descriptions.
