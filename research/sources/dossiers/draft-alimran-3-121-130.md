# Āl ʿImrān 3:121–130 — unpublished historical research notes

**Status:** Preliminary screening only (2026-11-05). Not a verified Sources-card dossier. No assertion of textual dependence.

## Historical setting and verse mapping

3:121–129 discuss the Prophet's positioning of believers, battle morale, Badr (3:123), heavenly aid (3:124–125), and divine judgment. Badr is an early Islamic historical event, **not** a pre-Islamic event with a corresponding earlier historical account. Do not attach earlier battle accounts to 3:121–123 as if they narrate Badr. 3:130 turns to an injunction against consuming multiplied usury; it is a distinct legal comparison.

## Source chronology, editions and witnesses

The Hebrew Bible passages belong to pre-Islamic textual traditions, but specific compositional layers, extant Hebrew witnesses and translation/edition choices must be documented before public verification. 2 Maccabees is a pre-Islamic Greek Jewish work; precise date and earliest extant manuscript witness must be checked separately. No claim is made that a surviving manuscript predates the Qur'an merely because the work's composition does.

## Interpretation and limitations

The angelic battle comparisons are motifs, not event-specific or demonstrably literary parallels. An alternative explanation is independently recurring religious warfare imagery. The usury comparison belongs to shared ancient Near Eastern and scriptural legal discourse, not necessarily a direct textual quotation. Consult relevant critical editions and scholarly studies before any dependence argument. No Arabic/Greek/Hebrew collation has been completed for this batch.
