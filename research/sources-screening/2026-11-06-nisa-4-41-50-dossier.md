# An-Nisāʾ 4:41–50 — witness, scripture, and polemics (unpublished research dossier)

**Status:** preliminary; not public; reviewed 2026-11-06. **Batch:** 055. **Scope:** individual first-pass screening of 4:41–50. No critical edition or manuscript collation completed. No primary-text-verified Sources associations.

## Historical and literary setting

This Medinan passage shifts from testimony at judgment (4:41–42), to ritual prayer and purification (4:43), then addresses people described as having received scripture and their response to revelation (4:44–50). The polemical references to some Jews in 4:46 must not be generalized to all Jews or treated as independent historical proof of manuscript alteration. Qur'anic wording, later exegetical explanations and modern scholarly reconstructions are distinct evidence types.

## Transmission, versions, and chronology

Isaiah 43 belongs to the Hebrew prophetic book of Isaiah, commonly located by historical-critical scholarship within exilic Isaiah 40–55; Deuteronomy is part of the Torah and underwent a complex pre-Islamic literary history. Composition, redaction and oldest surviving manuscript witnesses are separate chronological questions. The current screening used the **English Standard Version** as a convenient passage locator, not a Hebrew critical edition; the Greek Septuagint, Dead Sea Scroll witnesses where extant, Masoretic traditions and later translations have **not** been checked. No manuscript date or textual variant should be inferred from these English web pages.

- **4:43:** intoxicants, prayer, ritual purity, water and dry ablution; potential comparative ritual-law investigation is outstanding, with no assigned source.
- **4:44–45:** scriptural addressees, error and divine protection; no precise earlier parallel established at this screening depth.
- **4:46:** claims about displaced words and speech; stage Deuteronomy 4:2 as contrast only. Do not equate the Qur'anic claim with demonstrated physical corruption of any extant manuscript.
- **4:47–48:** confirmation of previous scripture, warning and divine forgiveness; investigate contextual pre-Islamic claims only with traceable locators.
- **4:49–50:** self-purification and fabrication against God; no precise external parallel assigned.

## Competing readings and research needs

Interpretations of 4:46 differ on whether the distortion refers to oral expression, interpretive reframing, textual handling or combinations; examine the Arabic expression and its classical commentaries before classifying. For 4:43, compare the precise procedures and legal settings rather than labeling all ancient washings equivalent. The Hebrew text of Isaiah 43:10 and Deuteronomy 4:2, manuscript transmission, and any Syriac or Jewish reception parallels remain unverified.
