# An-Nisāʾ 4:51–60 — scripture, judgment, authority (unpublished research dossier)

**Review date:** 2026-11-06. **Status:** preliminary; NOT public, NOT a comprehensive historical dossier. **Scope:** ten distinct ayat. Source cards must remain hidden until verified-only gate is demonstrated.

## Passage outline
4:51–55 polemic against certain interlocutors and invocation of Abrahamic scripture/kingdom; 4:56–57 contrast punishment and reward; 4:58–60 trusts, just judgment, authority and disputed arbitration. Do not map one generic biblical passage onto all ten verses.

The Hebrew expressions **מִשְׁפַּט־צֶדֶק** (just judgment, Deut 16:18) and **צֶדֶק צֶדֶק תִּרְדֹּף** (pursue justice, 16:20) are relevant to Qur'anic **بِٱلْعَدْلِ** (with justice, 4:58). These are different lexemes and language traditions, not evidence of quotation. The Deuteronomy passage also explicitly prohibits bribes (16:19), while 4:58 emphasizes restoring trusts. No claim of direct transmission is made.

## Chronology and transmission cautions
Deuteronomy is a pre-Islamic Hebrew biblical book, but its literary composition, editorial history and surviving textual witnesses are distinct questions. The linked Revised JPS 2023 is a **modern translation**, not a manuscript dated 2023. This batch did not collate Masoretic, Samaritan Pentateuch, Greek Septuagint or Dead Sea Scroll witnesses; no manuscript-based priority or verbatim identity claim is justified. Qur'anic text is viewed via [Quran.com](https://quran.com/en/an-nisa/58-61) and [legacy Quran.com 4:51–99](https://legacy.quran.com/4/51-99), not through a critical Qur'anic manuscript apparatus.

## Interpretive alternatives
The shared judicial norm may represent a broadly attested monotheistic ethical convention, independent moral reasoning, or participation in a wider late-antique scriptural discourse. These alternatives cannot be ranked from this comparison alone. The verses' specific polemical terms **jibt** and **taghut** and eschatological imagery require separate source-specific study; no unverified generic parallels are promoted.
