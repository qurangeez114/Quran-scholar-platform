## Narrative transition and source-specific candidate

Qur’an **11:25** introduces Noah as a messenger who gives a clear warning to his people (Arabic: *innī lakum nadhīrun mubīn*). The immediately following verse 11:26 specifies the content of his monotheistic warning. This is not the same speech situation as **Genesis 6:13**, where God speaks to Noah about impending destruction, followed by instructions for an ark in 6:14–16. The earlier account shares the named figure and pre-flood judgment setting, but not an explicit Noah-to-community warning speech in the compared passage. **Classification: shared narrative setting with significant difference**, not a direct quotation or established borrowing.

**Transmission and dating caveat:** Hebrew Bible composition, redaction, translation, and surviving manuscript witness dates are different questions. This screening does not date the Pentateuch's composition or inspect individual manuscript witnesses. The modern digital Hebrew/JPS presentation is not itself an ancient manuscript. Later Christian retellings (including 2 Peter 2:5) and earlier Mesopotamian flood accounts require separate source-specific checking before being added; do not infer a direct link from a shared flood motif.
